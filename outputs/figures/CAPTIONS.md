## Fig. 1 · The Andean barrier and its gateways

**Caption.**
(a) The W–E continental divide of the Andes (Copernicus GLO-30), coloured by crest elevation. Circles mark cols with depth ≥ 200 m, sized by depth. The ten lowest non-terminal tropical gateways are labelled with their crossing heights. The dashed line marks 23.5°S.
(b) Crest elevation (grey) and the lowest W–E crossing height per 0.25° of latitude (black), from 11°N to 55°S. Blue dots are gateways (the lowest col within ±100 km). Shaded bands are montane gaps, where the crossing height falls below 2000 m.
(c) Crossing height against isolation along the divide (log scale) for the 1380 tropical cols with depth ≥ 100 m that are not at the ends of the chain. Isolation is the distance along the divide to the nearest lower point. The five lowest and the five most isolated tropical gateways are labelled.

**Alt text.** Map of the Andean divide with labelled low passes, a latitudinal elevation profile, and a scatter of col height against isolation. The lowest tropical gateways (Táchira, Aguas Claras, Meta-Huila, Porculla) are also among the most isolated.

## Fig. 2 · Janzen's hypothesis and the hydric dimension along the Andes

**Caption.**
(a) Thermal overlap between crest and flank (Janzen overlap of the annual bio6–bio5 range, worse side, flank 1000 m) against |latitude| for the 61 gateways that are not at the ends of the chain. Points are coloured by crossing height. Lines show the GAM fixed effects at crossing heights of 1.5 and 3.5 km (the divide smooth is excluded), drawn over the latitudes of gateways within 0.5 km of each height and up to 1, the index maximum (dotted).
(b) Overlap against crossing height, with fixed-effect curves at 5° and 30° latitude, over the heights of tropical and temperate gateways and up to 1.
(c) Absolute aridity gap, |log(AI crest / AI flank)|, the larger of the two sides, against thermal overlap for the 30 tropical gateways. Dashed lines are the medians, which define the four types (cuts fixed before classification): open, closed in moisture only, closed in temperature only and closed.
Confidence intervals of the model terms are given in Table 2.

**Alt text.** Thermal overlap rises with latitude and falls with col height, more steeply in the tropics. Among tropical gateways the absolute aridity gap is only weakly related to thermal overlap; the gateways that are thermally open but hydrically closed all lie on the Altiplano (16–23°S).

## Fig. 3 · Future permeability

**Caption.**
(a) Change from the present (1970–2000) in the thermal overlap of the future crest with the present flank, and in the precipitation gap, log(P crest future / P flank present), taken as the larger absolute value of the two sides so that a positive change means a wider contrast, for the 30 tropical gateways, by period and SSP. Points are the mean of 8 CMIP6 GCMs (WorldClim 2.1 deltas) and bars the GCM range. Filled points: at least 7 of 8 GCMs agree on the sign of the change.
(b) ΔT\*, the crest warming that would centre the crest's annual range on that of the present flank, against crossing height (r = 0.97); the five lowest gateways are labelled.
(c) Fraction of ΔT\* reached in 2081–2100 (mean and GCM range) by SSP, on a log scale. Crosses mark gateways whose future crest is warmer than every present lowland cell within 100 km in more than half of the GCMs. Táchira has the smallest ΔT\* (1.0 °C) and overshoots it 1.9–5.4 times.

**Alt text.** Thermal overlap increases robustly at 29 of 30 tropical gateways under every scenario, and decreases only at Táchira. Precipitation-gap changes are small and their sign is robust at no more than half of the gateways.

## Fig. 4 · Andean connectivity under climate change: where to act

**Caption.**
(a) Tropical Andean divide classified by the montane corridor: kept (blue), lost by 2081–2100 under SSP3-7.0 in at least 7 of 8 GCMs (vermilion), and montane gap today (grey; crossing height < 2000 m). Green: protected areas (WDPA, November 2026). Triangles mark critical gateways, those where the lowland corridor opens (≥ 0.5 of ΔT\* reached) or where the montane corridor is lost within ±25 km. Black triangles also have high human modification at the crest (gHM ≥ 0.4). No critical gateway has its crest inside a protected area.
(b) Length of the tropical divide in montane gaps, plotted at period midpoints. Future gaps are crossings below today's 2000 m isotherm, raised by the local warming divided by the local lapse rate. Lines: mean of 8 GCMs; bands: GCM range.
(c) Share of the montane corridor that is kept or lost, inside protected areas and under high human modification.

**Alt text.** Montane gaps along the tropical Andes triple by 2090 under SSP3-7.0. The losses are in Colombia (123 km), northern Peru (103 km), Venezuela (81 km, 18 % of its montane divide) and southern Ecuador (4 km); none south of 6.6°S. The stretches lost are as poorly protected as the rest of the corridor but almost three times more human-modified, and the six critical gateways are all unprotected.

## Fig. 5 · Synthesis: the controls of climatic permeability, shown on real Andean relief

Synthesis figure, first cited in the Discussion; it summarizes results already shown in Figs. 1–4. Generated by `scripts/32_fig5_sintesis.R`: rayshader blocks from the HydroSHEDS 15" DEM with data drapes, composed in SVG and exported with Inkscape (PDF with text as paths, PNG 600 dpi).

**Caption.**
(a) Janzen's prediction at two gateways of similar height, one tropical (0.6°S, col 3496 m) and one temperate (34.2°S, col 3420 m). Relief (HydroSHEDS) is coloured by mean annual temperature (WorldClim 2.1). Bars give the annual temperature range, from the mean minimum of the coldest month to the mean maximum of the warmest month, of the lowland flank at 1000 m and of the crest, on the side with the lower overlap. The two ranges are 1.6 °C apart in the tropics and overlap by 10.5 °C in the temperate zone. (b) The moisture axis: aridity index (Global-AI v3.1) across an Altiplano gateway (17.7°S, col 4257 m), where the Pacific flank in the Atacama is about 32 times drier than the crest and the eastern flank, the nearest lowland cell about 250 km from the col rather than the end of a descent path, about 4.6 times wetter, and across the Táchira depression (7.7°N, col 1023 m), where crest and flanks are equally humid. (c) The tropical divide from 0.6°N to 10°S seen from the west, coloured by whether the ground lies below (lowland climate) or above (montane climate) the isotherm that now lies at 2000 m. Under SSP3-7.0 in 2081–2100 this isotherm rises by a median of 735 m along the tropical divide (mean of eight CMIP6 models), and that median rise is applied to the whole area shown. Low crests pass into lowland climate, which opens the west–east crossing and breaks the north–south montane belt at the same places; high crests keep montane climate. Thin lines in c are national borders (GADM 4.1). Numbered markers (1 to 4) link the passes in a and b to the inset map, which also outlines the area shown in c. Vertical exaggeration 2.2 in a, 3 to 4 in b and 5 in c.

**Alt text.** Three rows of shaded 3D relief. Top: a tropical and a temperate pass of similar height, with bars showing that the annual temperature range of the lowland flank does not reach the tropical crest but overlaps the temperate one. Middle: the Altiplano crossing grades from hyper-arid brown on the Pacific side to green on the eastern slope, while Táchira is green throughout. Bottom: the tropical divide today and in 2081–2100; the green montane belt shrinks to the high crests, with new breaks at the low crests.
